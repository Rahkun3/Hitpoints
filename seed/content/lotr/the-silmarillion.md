Published in 1977, *The Silmarillion* was compiled and edited by **Christopher Tolkien** from his father’s manuscripts, with **Guy Gavriel Kay**’s editorial assistance commonly noted. It is not a novel: a summary of the Elder Days plus framing.

Parts in order: **Ainulindalë** (creation), **Valaquenta** (Valar and Maiar), **Quenta Silmarillion** (First Age history of the Silmarils and the Noldor — largely tragic), **Akallabêth** (Númenor), and **Of the Rings of Power and the Third Age** (the book-side bridge into *The Lord of the Rings*).

It is denser than *The Hobbit* or *The Lord of the Rings*: many names, a tragic mode, and no novel pacing. Read it after LotR, with a family tree or map handy. Galadriel appears; the Rings chapter is the literary lead-in to LotR. Jackson’s films barely use this material (the LotR prologue is a sketch of the Last Alliance). Amazon’s Second Age series draws names from this era but is a **Series** track, not a *Silmarillion* adaptation.

For fuller tellings of single First Age tales, see the later volumes *The Children of Húrin*, *Beren and Lúthien*, and *The Fall of Gondolin*.
