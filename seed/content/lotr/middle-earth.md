In Tolkien’s books, **Middle-earth** is the principal inhabited region of **Arda**, the world of the legendarium. The published core is *The Hobbit* (1937), *The Lord of the Rings* (1954–55), and *The Silmarillion* (1977, edited by Christopher Tolkien from his father’s manuscripts), with *Unfinished Tales* (1980) and later edited volumes expanding the same myth. Those books are **literary canon**.

Peter Jackson’s films (*The Lord of the Rings*, 2001–03; *The Hobbit*, 2012–14) are a **Film** track: a screen adaptation, not a replacement for the novels. Amazon’s *The Rings of Power* is Second Age television on a separate **Series** track; it is not Jackson continuity. Licensed games — notably *Middle-earth: Shadow of Mordor* and *Shadow of War*, and *The Lord of the Rings Online* — tell their own stories and are not legendarium canon.

Every article carries a continuity badge (**Book**, **Film**, **Series**, **Game**, or **Shared**). Names and places that travel across tracks use Shared without claiming one plot. First-time readers should start with the Reading Order guide.
