In Tolkien's books, Mordor, the Black Land, lies east of the Anduin and of Gondor, walled by the Ephel Dúath on the west and south and the Ered Lithui on the north. The Morannon (Black Gate) opens into Udûn; another pass is the Cirith Ungol / Minas Morgul route. The plateau of Gorgoroth is ash and war. Barad-dûr was founded in the Second Age, thrown down after the Last Alliance, and rebuilt in the Third. Orodruin (Mount Doom) is where the One Ring was forged; in the books it is already there when Sauron occupies Mordor in the Second Age. Southern Nurn around Lake Núrnen is agricultural, worked by slaves — Mordor is not only volcano.

In Jackson's films, Mordor is a volcanic wasteland. The Eye of Sauron as a literal searchlight on Barad-dûr is Film language; in the books the Eye is chiefly a metaphor and emblem of Sauron's will, with some visionary scenes.

In Amazon's series, the Southlands eruption creates Orodruin (Mount Doom) and the ashen land of Mordor — Series, not a Book account of how the volcano or plateau formed. *Shadow of Mordor* and *Shadow of War* are open-world Game stories (Celebrimbor, the Bright Lord) on a non-canon Game track.
