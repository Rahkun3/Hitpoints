In Tolkien's books, Sauron as Annatar taught ring-craft in Eregion in the Second Age. Celebrimbor and the Gwaith-i-Mírdain made the Seven and the Nine under Sauron's guidance; Celebrimbor alone made the Three. Sauron forged the One in Orodruin to rule the others. The Three were not touched by his hand but were still subject while he wore the One. The Ring bears an inscription in Black Speech. Isildur cut it from Sauron at the Last Alliance, would not destroy it, and lost it in the Anduin. Déagol found it; Sméagol took it and became Gollum. Bilbo found it under the Misty Mountains; Frodo inherited it. It makes mortal wearers vanish by shifting them toward the Unseen ("invisibility" is the mortal perception), extends life, and dominates the will. Destroyed only in that fire; Gollum and the fire end it.

In Jackson's films, the *Fellowship of the Ring* prologue dramatizes the Last Alliance; the Ring is treated as a whispering character. The Eye as a searchlight or lidless-eye visual is Film; in the books the Eye is chiefly a metaphor and emblem of Sauron's will and presence, with some visionary scenes. Putting on the Ring is shown as an immediate wraith-world overlay.

In Amazon's series, Second Age ring-forging uses Series order and inventions — mithril-origin of the Three, the Three forged first then the Seven and Nine, and Halbrand then Annatar — not a substitute for Of the Rings of Power in *The Silmarillion*. The One has not been forged on screen as of Season 2; Season 3 is unaired. *Shadow of Mordor* and *Shadow of War* recast Celebrimbor as wraith co-forger and Bright Lord — Game invention, not Book.
